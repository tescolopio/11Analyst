# Paddle technique reference for shot categories

This reference maps each spin type to the paddle adjustments needed to counter it.
These are coaching principles, not measured paddle angles from telemetry.

## Core principle

**Angle the paddle face into the spin direction to neutralize it.**

The ball's spin affects how it rebounds off your paddle:
- Spin "grabs" your paddle face and deflects the ball in the spin direction
- To counter this, angle your paddle face against the spin before contact

---

## Spin type counters

### Topspin (rotation: +X in receiver frame)

**Ball behavior:** Kicks upward off your paddle, tends to go long.

**Counter:**
- Close paddle face (tilt forward/down)
- Use a compact block or counter-drive
- Reduce upward lift compared to a flat ball

**Common errors:**
- Paddle too open → ball flies long
- Adding unnecessary lift → overshooting the table

---

### Backspin (rotation: -X in receiver frame)

**Ball behavior:** Dies on contact, drops into the net if not lifted.

**Counter:**
- Open paddle face (tilt back/up)
- Add controlled lift with brushing contact
- More lift needed for heavier backspin

**Common errors:**
- Paddle too closed → ball goes into net
- Treating as flat ball → insufficient lift

---

### Sidespin - Clockwise viewed from above (rotation: -Y)

**Ball behavior:** Curves left toward receiver, kicks left off paddle.

**Counter:**
- Angle paddle face to the RIGHT before contact
- Aim slightly right of intended target
- Combine with top/back adjustment if mixed spin

**Visual cue:** Ball curves toward your backhand side (if right-handed).

---

### Sidespin - Counterclockwise viewed from above (rotation: +Y)

**Ball behavior:** Curves right toward receiver, kicks right off paddle.

**Counter:**
- Angle paddle face to the LEFT before contact
- Aim slightly left of intended target
- Combine with top/back adjustment if mixed spin

**Visual cue:** Ball curves toward your forehand side (if right-handed).

---

### Sidespin + Topspin (side_top)

**Ball behavior:** Curves sideways AND kicks up.

**Counter:**
- Close paddle face (for topspin component)
- Angle paddle into sidespin direction
- Compact block/counter with sideways compensation
- Less lift than pure sidespin, more angle than pure topspin

---

### Sidespin + Backspin (side_back)

**Ball behavior:** Curves sideways AND dies on contact.

**Counter:**
- Open paddle face (for backspin component)
- Angle paddle into sidespin direction
- Lift with sideways aim compensation
- Common on short serves - requires delicate touch

---

### Negligible spin (none)

**Ball behavior:** Rebounds predictably without spin deflection.

**Counter:**
- Neutral paddle angle
- Focus on placement and timing
- Do NOT add spin compensation - will cause errors

**Trap:** Assuming a serve has no spin when it actually does.

## Length-specific adjustments

| Length | Position | Stroke options |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Short | Move in close to table | Touch, flick, or push depending on spin |
| Half-long | Judgment call | If clears end line → drive; if not → touch |
| Long | Create space behind | Drive, loop, or controlled counter |
| Deep | Don't get crowded | Step back, compact stroke |

---

## Placement-specific footwork

| Placement | Movement | Recovery |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Backhand side | Pivot or step to backhand | Return to ready position |
| Forehand side | Step out to forehand | Wide balls need extra recovery |
| Table middle | Decide stroke early | Elbow decision point |
| Wide backhand | Move laterally, don't just reach | Immediate recovery needed |
| Wide forehand | Step out fully | Longest recovery distance |

---

## Diagnostic process

When returns fail repeatedly:

1. **Identify the spin type** - Watch ball rotation, observe bounce behavior
2. **Check paddle angle** - Is it countering the spin or amplifying it?
3. **Separate error types:**
   - Net errors → likely need more lift (backspin) or more closed face (topspin)
   - Long errors → likely need less lift or more closed face
   - Side errors → sidespin compensation needed
4. **Adjust one thing at a time** - Don't change angle AND aim simultaneously

## Mapping to shot catalogue categories

| Category | Primary counter | Secondary adjustment |
|----------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Long sidespin serve (backhand) | Angle into sidespin direction | Lift/close based on top/back component |
| Short backspin serve (backhand) | Open face, gentle lift | Move in close, touch or flick |
| Short backspin serve (forehand) | Open face, gentle lift | Step out to forehand position |
| Half-long side-back serve (middle) | Open + sideways angle | Read length before committing |
| Long topspin serve (backhand) | Close face, compact block | Create space, don't reach |
| Long backspin serve (forehand) | Open face, drive with lift | Step out, controlled opening |
| Short no-spin serve (middle) | Neutral angle | Focus on placement, no compensation |
| Deep topspin rally (backhand) | Close face, counter-drive | Don't get crowded |
| Deep topspin rally (forehand) | Close face, counter-drive | Good footwork to forehand |
| Deep backspin rally (backhand) | Open face, loop or push | Read spin weight |
| Mid-depth side-top rally (wide FH) | Close + sideways angle | Move to ball, recover |
| Short backspin rally (forehand) | Open face, touch or flick | Move in quickly |

## Validation status

These are coaching principles based on table tennis fundamentals. They are **not derived
from telemetry measurements** of your actual paddle angles during play.

To validate whether you're applying these counters correctly:
1. Record practice sessions with known spin types
2. Note which returns succeed vs fail
3. Check if failures match the predicted error pattern for incorrect technique
4. Adjust and re-test

The spin conventions (+X = topspin, -X = backspin, +/-Y = sidespin direction) match the
shot catalogue's illustrative receiver frame but are **not yet validated** against
actual log coordinates.
